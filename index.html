Featured research

Liquidation-cascade signal for maker fills

BTC/ETH perpetual futures Dec 2025 – Feb 2026 data Independent, 2026
Figure pack (PDF) ↗ 80 pages · 16.5 MB · cascade phases, episodes and daily P&L

When forced liquidations cascade through a perpetual-futures market, some passive (maker) fills get run over and some get paid. The question: can causal features of the cascade tell them apart in real time, and does the difference survive trading costs?

  1. Data. Streamed ~1.3B trades into day-sharded Parquet on integer-microsecond clocks, correcting Bybit’s 200 ms feed latency before any feature is computed. Found Binance’s liquidation feed is throttled to one row per second per symbol, so it under-reports exactly during cascades; re-used it for sign and timing only and took size from Bybit.
  2. Features. ~80 strictly causal features: Hawkes intensities of forced flow, cascade state, price displacement since cascade start (IC ≈ 0.14), order-flow imbalance, BTC–ETH lead–lag, forced-flow VPIN. Each passed brute-force recomputation and truncation tests with zero mismatches.
  3. Selection. A pre-registered keep/drop ledger removed 39 features. A model ladder from threshold rules to ridge, monotone GBM and LightGBM ran on identical 28-day walk-forward folds with a 1-day embargo, Bonferroni-adjusted statistics and a Hansen SPA check. A three-feature composite beat LightGBM out of sample, 5.1 vs 3.4 bps. A side-asymmetry hypothesis was dropped when its sign reversed out of sample.
  4. Holdout. The frozen specification, keeping the top 5% of maker-fill turnover, ran once on 14 unseen days: +5.1 bps over the unfiltered fill set at 120 s, day-bootstrap 5% lower bound +2.4 bps, 82% of coin-days positive, random placebo ≈ 0.
  5. Tradability. A queue-position and exit simulator on a 1-second order-book panel tested whether the edge can be captured after costs.

Options order-book research

EUCO options vs FCEU futures · 22 sessions, Mar–Apr 2026

35M market-by-order events replayed into books · descriptive research, no live orders

Rebuilt bid and ask books from add, cancel and modify events, mapped a volatility surface (106 usable IV observations across 6 expiries) and tested skew, liquidity and time-of-day effects. Realised-volatility timing was consistent across slots (rank consistency 0.85) but liquidity was not (−0.34), so timing supports hedge-overlay research rather than a profit claim. The original momentum hypothesis was rejected.
